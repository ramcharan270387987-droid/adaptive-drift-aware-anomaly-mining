# Difficult faculty questions — 16 answers

Lead with the tested result, then name the comparator, dataset, or measurement boundary.

1. **Is your contribution actually novel?** ADWIN, Z-score, and adaptation are established ideas. The contribution is the specified gating policy, matched controls, sealed comparisons, and a working system that exposes why an individual refit occurred. The study makes no priority claim. [Related work](../IEEE_MANUSCRIPT.md#ii-related-work)
2. **Why didn’t you use an LSTM or Transformer?** This experiment isolates the update timing and cost of an interpretable reference model. A sequence model would add representation and training choices that need their own locked protocol. The study makes no comparative claim about neural detectors. [Objectives and baselines](../PROJECT_REPORT.md#7-objectives-and-contributions)
3. **Why use Z-score for adaptation?** Refitting its mean and scale is cheap to measure, easy to explain, and supports an exact comparison against the frozen version. The resulting conclusions are about that policy and reference model. [Frozen update policy](../selective_adaptation_protocol.md)
4. **What if ADWIN interprets an anomaly as drift?** ADWIN alarms can be wrong. Two median blocks must agree before refitting, and the full policy filters flagged values under a retained-count rule. The sealed gradual failure example still had an unmatched alarm at 2047 and an unmatched refit at 2239. [Failure cases](../final_evaluation.md#failure-cases)
5. **Why did fixed scheduling outperform your method in some cases?** A regular refit can catch changing recent data without waiting on persistence and filtering. On synthetic holdout it produced 24,872 post-drift false positives versus 56,768 for full selective, with 49 more misses and much more refitting. Which trade-off is preferable depends on the application. [Final synthetic results](../final_evaluation.md#primary-synthetic-holdout)
6. **Why should we trust the synthetic results?** Paired traces share the base signal, and the independent planted truth permits direct comparison of the same events. The protocol and holdout were frozen, with uncertainty estimated across seed blocks. This supports an internally controlled comparison under the stated distribution assumptions. [Design and statistics](../final_evaluation.md#prespecified-statistical-comparisons)
7. **Why didn’t the benefit generalize to NAB?** The full policy made one extra exact detection and added 11,288 point false positives, mostly on machine temperature. Five series and sparse labels limit interpretation, and the benchmark lacks independent drift truth. The outcome establishes the trade-off on these files without identifying a unique cause. [Final NAB](../final_evaluation.md#final-nab)
8. **Does Spark improve detection accuracy?** The measured workloads reproduced independent replay exactly. Spark supports execution, state recovery, and reproducibility. The predictive differences come from the policy and data. [Implementation](../ARCHITECTURE.md)
9. **Is 3,886 rows/s scalable?** It is the highest observed commit rate under a finite single-host trial with eight streams and 0.25-second triggers. The heavier workload with shorter triggers reached 21,300 peak queued rows. Long-run capacity and distributed scaling are unmeasured. [Benchmark](../SUSTAINED_STREAMING.md)
10. **Why is this data mining?** The work searches for point deviations and sustained changes in sequential data, evaluates them against independent evidence, and compares how alternative discovery and update rules behave. Machine learning estimators are tools within that evaluation.
11. **How would you deploy this in production?** I would add durable, partitioned ingestion, preserve order by stream key, use a scalable checkpoint and sink strategy, and benchmark restart, lag, backpressure, and alert quality on realistic traffic. The tested implementation runs locally with an ordered file source and SQLite. [Architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md)
12. **What happens with multivariate data?** The current detector accepts scalar values. A multivariate extension could track correlations or a vector-derived score, with tests for covariance drift and marginal drift. Its accuracy and update behavior would require a new evaluation. [Study limitations](../final_evaluation.md#limitations)
13. **What happens under recurrent drift?** The policy refits one reference at a time and can take time to confirm a return. In one clean recurring holdout trace it produced 124 false positives in the 256 points after returning to the shifted regime. [Recurring failure](../final_evaluation.md#failure-cases)
14. **How do you avoid catastrophic adaptation?** Persistence, filtered candidates, a minimum sample, update budgets, and cooldown limit abrupt or repeated refits. They do not guarantee protection. The study saw delayed and unmatched updates and a predictor can still learn an unsuitable reference. [Failure cases and matching](../final_evaluation.md#failure-cases)
15. **What would you change with another semester?** First I would improve validation with more real series and independently annotated regime changes. Then I would compare calibrated adaptive thresholds and multivariate policies on a fresh holdout. Kafka and distributed soak and recovery testing are separate engineering work. [Future work](../PROJECT_REPORT.md#27-future-work)
16. **Why not promote secondary persistence to the primary result after seeing its lower FP?** The comparison was frozen with full selective versus static Z-score as primary. Persistence had fewer synthetic post-drift flags but lower recall than full and more point false positives than static on final NAB. Promoting it after seeing the results would undermine the confirmatory design. [Prespecified comparisons](../final_evaluation.md#prespecified-statistical-comparisons)
